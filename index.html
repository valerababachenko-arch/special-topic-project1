Workshop 1

Executive function

Action Research

The action research evaluated tools, platforms, and workflows to determine the most feasible and professionally relevant approach for building a motion-driven 3D experience, including software comparison, web integration testing, and subscription considerations.

  • Defined the action research focus as evaluating tools, platforms, and workflows for a motion-driven, scroll-based interactive 3D storytelling website concept.
  • Compared key 3D production tools (Spline, Blender, Cinema 4D, Unity) to understand strengths, limitations, learning curve, and best fit for web-based interactive storytelling.
  • Researched practical web integration by testing how Spline can be embedded into Framer (Embed component and <spline-viewer>), supporting scroll and interaction events.
  • Investigated platform constraints affecting deployment, including Framer Student Plans one-site publishing limit and a workaround using a separate student account for publishing.
  • Reviewed Spline subscription tiers and AI 3D generation capabilities (text-to-3D and image-to-3D) to assess whether upgrading could improve asset creation and final quality.

Project Concept

Motion-driven 3D interactive storytelling website

Research Reflection

After completing my research phase and receiving peer feedback, I took time to critically reflect on my initial concept. Although the motion-driven storytelling direction was conceptually strong and well-researched, I realized that I was not genuinely excited about continuing in that direction. I experimented with a few early visual and structural ideas, but the process did not feel motivating or creatively engaging for me.

I understood that while a concept may be academically valid and strategically sound, it also needs to align with personal interest and long-term portfolio goals. I began reassessing what type of work I am most passionate about and what direction would better represent the kind of designer I want to become.

As a result, I have decided to shift my project toward creating a 3D UI or product animation using Blender or After Effects. I am particularly drawn to the intersection of interface design, motion, and spatial depth.

During my research, I found several examples of product launch animations and 3D UI explorations that clearly demonstrate the level of polish and intentional motion I would like to achieve. These references helped clarify both the aesthetic direction and technical standard I want to pursue. In the following slides, I will present visual examples that represent the quality, tone, and complexity I aim to accomplish.

This shift in direction reflects not a lack of commitment, but rather a more refined understanding of my interests and strengths.

Next Steps

  1. Refine the Product Concept

    Clearly define the type of product, the target user, and the core features that will be highlighted in the animation.

  2. Create Low-Fidelity Storyboards

    Sketch the structure of the promo video, mapping out scene transitions, camera movement, feature highlights, and motion hierarchy before building in 3D.

  3. Develop a Functional Motion Prototype

    Build a simplified version of key UI animations using Blender or After Effects, focusing on:

    • Camera depth
    • UI layering
    • Microinteractions
    • Transition pacing
  4. Conduct Usability & Perception Testing

    Share the prototype with peers to test:

    • Clarity of feature communication
    • Readability of UI elements
    • Visual hierarchy
    • Overall professional feel
  5. Evaluate Motion Effectiveness

    Assess whether motion enhances understanding or distracts from the interface. Identify moments where pacing or clarity needs refinement.

  6. Iterate Based on Feedback

    Refine animations, simplify overly complex sequences, and improve visual balance before moving into full production.

  7. Document Testing Outcomes

    Record insights, challenges, and improvements to demonstrate evidence-based design decisions.
